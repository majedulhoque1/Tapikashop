Who we are for

A son in Kathmandu choosing something for his didi in Melbourne. A family in Pokhara remembering a birthday in Sydney. People who want to be present at a festival they cannot attend.

What we are

One person who buys, assembles, wraps and hand-delivers in Australia, paid for through eSewa. Small enough to care, organised enough to be trusted. We never pretend to be a warehouse.

Type

Young Serif for headlines: round, open, friendly, close to the hand-lettered logo. Mukta for everything you read: it draws Latin and Devanagari together, so Nepali never looks like a pasted-in guest.

Send love.

Young Serif 400 (single weight), display. Tight leading 1.04 to 1.1, tracking -2%.

Radius, one rule

Pill for what you press, 12px for what you type in, 20px for what holds a picture. Nothing else.

Shadows are tinted cocoa, never grey-black, and only on lifted things (hovered buttons, sheets). Cards are a last resort: group with space and a dashed twine line first.

Motion

Things arrive like a parcel set down gently: fast start, long soft landing. Hover or tap the box to compare.

cubic-bezier(.16, 1, .3, 1) for arrivals and reveals. cubic-bezier(.65, 0, .35, 1) for things that travel. The spring curve is for one tiny confirmation, like a tick landing.

Animate transform and opacity only. Stagger 70 ms. Every sequence collapses to its end state under reduced motion. No looping animation anywhere except a loading skeleton.

Photography

The gift is the hero. Natural window light, one product sharp in frame, props from the table: kraft paper, marigold garland, navy card stock, gold foil, chocolate.

4:5

Product and Instagram

Hero item sharp, soft side light, one garland edge or ribbon at most.

1:1

Grid and cart thumbnails

Centred product on kraft or blush paper. Same light direction across a set.

16:9

Hero and banners

Compose for 16:9 from the start. Never crop the product to fit.

Daylight white balance, no flash, no heavy filters or presets. Rakhi red and marigold must read true. Hands holding a gift are welcome, faces are not (see right).

Personalised frames: privacy first

Never show a customer's face, name, date or message from a personalised photo frame without their written consent. This applies to the site, Instagram, ads and the Command Center demo data.

Show the frame with the owner's own sample prints, a blank mat, or a softly blurred image. Reviews are real text, anonymised ("A customer in Sydney"), and never invented. Alt text describes the product, never a person.

  • One gift per frame, shot at its real colour and scale.
  • Wrapping and card visible: the personal touch is the product.
  • Compress at upload, lazy-load below the fold, keep the original.
  • Stock photos of strangers hugging in airports.
  • Text, logos or price tags laid over the product.
  • Another brand's packaging in frame.

Voice

Warm, plain English with Nepali in it where Nepali is the natural word: Didi, Dai, Aama, Tika, Tihar. Short sentences, "you" and "your", one exclamation mark per page at most. The sender is speaking to someone they love, so we speak to the sender like a trusted cousin who happens to live in Australia.
