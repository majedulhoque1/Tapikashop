EVA'S GIFT SHOP — COMPLETE EXPERIUS SYSTEM BLUEPRINT
The working product concept


Customer-facing idea

Send a little love to Australia.

A gifting service that allows people in Nepal to send thoughtful gifts to loved ones anywhere in Australia, with Eva handling the Australian-side purchasing, preparation, packaging and delivery.

Internal Experius concept

Gifting Experience + Fulfillment OS

Not:

Instagram → DM → Manual Order

But:

Discover → Choose → Personalize → Pay → Order → Fulfill → Deliver → Remember

STEP 1 — BUSINESS DISCOVERY & CURRENT-STATE DIAGNOSTIC

Before designing anything, we need to understand how Eva's business actually works today.

This is the most important step because we're not going to impose a system on her.

We're going to uncover the system that's already hiding inside her business.

1.1 Understand the business

During the session with Eva, establish:

Business basics
What is the business currently called?
How long has she been doing it?
Where do customers come from?
How many orders does she receive?
What occasions are most common?
What products sell most?
What types of bundles does she already create?
What areas of Australia does she deliver to?
How does she currently calculate delivery fees?
How far in advance do people usually order?
What is the average order value?
What percentage are repeat customers?
What percentage are custom requests?

We should specifically investigate:

The key diagnostic question:

"What parts of this business require Eva's judgment, and what parts simply require Eva's time?"

We automate the second category.

1.4 Separate the HUMAN layer from the SYSTEM layer

This is extremely important to the Experius philosophy.

Eva should remain responsible for:
Curation
Taste
Quality control
Personal touch
Exceptional/custom requests
Gift presentation
Customer relationships
Final fulfillment decisions
The system should handle:
Product discovery
Order collection
Customer information
Recipient information

Order status
Gift composition
Procurement lists
Delivery schedules
Notifications
Customer history
Basic analytics

So:

We don't remove Eva from the business. We remove unnecessary work from Eva.

STEP 2 — V1 BUSINESS MODEL & POSITIONING

Now we turn the diagnostic into a clear business model.

2.1 Positioning

Don't position this as:

"An online gift shop."

That's too generic.

Instead:

A personal gifting service that helps people in Nepal send thoughtful gifts to loved ones living in Australia.

The differentiator isn't necessarily the products.

It's the experience and trust.

2.2 Core value proposition
Customer promise

Choose a gift. Make it personal. We'll take care of the rest.

Potential supporting message:

You may be far away. Your gift doesn't have to feel that way.

2.3 Why would someone use Eva?

Because they:

live in Nepal
have someone they love in Australia
want to send something thoughtful
don't know the Australian market
don't want to deal with Australian purchasing
want convenient Nepal-side payment
want the gift beautifully prepared
want someone trustworthy to handle the delivery

That's the real problem.

2.4 Three customer modes

This becomes a foundational part of the website.

MODE 1 — SHOP GIFTS

"I know what I want."

Browse ready-made gifts.

MODE 2 — GIFT BUILDER

"I want to create something."

Choose multiple products and create a custom gift box, hamper, bag or small bundle.

MODE 3 — HELP ME CHOOSE

"I have no idea what to send."

A guided experience that asks:

Who?
Occasion?
Relationship?
Approximate budget?
Interests?
Style?

Then presents suitable options.

Eventually this could become an AI-assisted recommendation engine.

But not necessary for V1.

2.5 Business model

Conceptually:

NEPAL CUSTOMER
      ↓
Local Nepal Payment
      ↓
Eva's Nepal-side settlement
      ↓
Australian Product Purchase
      ↓
Packaging / Assembly
      ↓
Australian Delivery
      ↓
RECIPIENT

However, this must be validated with Eva before production regarding:

business registration
payment gateway eligibility
settlement
currency conversion
taxation
refunds
cross-border payment rules
Australian/Nepal business structure

For the prototype, none of this needs to block us.
